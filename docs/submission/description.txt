Mandate is a capital-allocation market for autonomous trading agents. An allocator funds an agent without handing over custody: the money sits in a vault contract, the agent holds one permission (execute), and every order must pass an on-chain risk guard before it reaches the venue. The question an allocator has to answer changes from "can I trust this trader?" to "can I accept these terms?".

THE PROBLEM

Someone who wants to back a trading agent needs three answers.

1. Can the agent take the money? With a managed account or an API key, the operator can trade and can also withdraw. Exchange copy-trading moves custody to the exchange, which changes who is trusted, not whether.
2. Can the agent exceed the risk it agreed to? Off chain the limits are a description, and the allocator learns they were exceeded after the loss. An agent trades around the clock and nobody reads every order.
3. Can vaults be compared without publishing every private expression of interest?

Mandate answers the first two on chain today. For the third, the accounting is built and the private part is not.

WHAT MANDATE DOES

A mandate is a set of terms written into a contract and locked before the first deposit: eleven risk limits, six trade terms and two fees, all bound into one termsHash.

- Open registration. MandateFactory lets anyone deploy a vault, set its terms, lock them and list it in one transaction. Nobody approves it. The guard and the factory refuse values outside their ranges.
- Custody separation. MandateVault holds the USDC and issues shares at marked NAV. The agent can call execute() and nothing else. A freeze stops the agent and leaves withdrawals open.
- Pre-trade limits. The venue adapter previews the exposure an order would leave. MandateRiskGuard checks order, position and total size, leverage, per-block notional, trade spacing, allowed markets, direction, limit-price distance from the mark and trades per day before any external call. An order that fails reverts and no venue state changes.
- Terms checked on the mark. After every trade the guard re-marks the vault. Drawdown against the high-water mark, loss since the day's open and holding time are checked there, and anyone can call poke() between trades to prove a breach. The vault freezes and the caller is paid a 0.05% bounty, so enforcement does not depend on a trusted keeper.
- Unwind after a freeze. Anyone can call unwind() to close a frozen position in five reduce-only steps of 20%, each inside a 1% slippage bound, for a 0.01% bounty. The vault ends Closed.
- Locked terms. lockTerms() is one-way. A vault refuses deposits until its terms are locked, and afterwards nothing in them can change.
- Volatility clause. From its own marks the guard estimates realised volatility and refuses an order that adds exposure (StressBreach) when a k-sigma move would push the vault past its drawdown limit. Reducing orders always pass.
- Mark age. A vault whose venue price is older than maxMarkAgeSeconds cannot trade or take deposits. If no price arrives for three times that age, anyone can freeze it, and allocators of a frozen vault can then take a cash-only exit (withdrawUnpriced) without waiting for the feed.
- Fees as terms. A management fee (at most 5% a year) and a performance fee (at most 30%, only above the fee high-water mark) are charged by the vault in new shares, on a fresh mark only.
- Batch allocation and releases. BatchAllocator takes EIP-712 signed intents with escrow, nets an epoch into one deposit per vault and pays shares against Merkle proofs. MandateRegistry anchors each agent's locked terms and accepts signed statistical releases, each stating the epsilon it spends, up to a cap.

WHY THIS NEEDS MONAD

The mark-age term has a floor, and the floor is the block interval. A mark is refreshed by a transaction, so when the next block's transactions read it, it is already one block interval old. A mandate that says "do not trust a price older than 4 seconds" can only be kept where blocks arrive inside 4 seconds. On 12-second blocks the mark has expired before the next block exists: a trade or deposit goes through only if it lands in the same block as an oracle update and behind it, and reverts with MarkTooOld in every later block.

The local build simulates this with a switch that holds the oracle to one mark per 12 seconds, the most a 12-second chain can deliver, while the local chain keeps mining. At 1-second cadence, orders on the Tight Mandate vault pass its 4-second term. At 12-second cadence the same order reverts with MarkTooOld for most of each interval.

The block interval is the floor, not the whole bound: the price feed has to arrive that often too. On Monad testnet our demo oracle is a paid transaction every 5 seconds, so the tightest live mandate asks for a 10-second mark. Eight consecutive poke() calls on that vault all passed the term, each checked against a mark 1 to 5 seconds old (read back from the chain; the README lists them). The 4-second term runs on the local build.

WHAT IS LIVE TODAY

- Contracts on Monad testnet (chain 10143): MandateFactory, MandateRiskGuard, four seeded MandateVaults with different mandates, BatchAllocator, MandateRegistry, the venue adapter, a mock venue and a mock USDC. Addresses are in the README.
- A web app with Market, Agent, Allocate, Batch, Privacy, Launch and Live Risk screens. Every number is a contract read and every button is a transaction. Each vault shows its term sheet: the locked value, where the vault stands and what a breach does. A visitor can use a demo allocator the server signs for, or a browser wallet with a test-USDC faucet.
- A demo video recorded on the hosted testnet demo: a mandate launched from a wallet, a deposit, an over-limit order refused, a price shock, poke() freezing the vault, the agent refused, unwind steps and a withdrawal.
- An adapter for Perpl, the perpetuals exchange on Monad, tested against Perpl's deployed exchange on a fork of Monad testnet: open, a guard refusal, a flip, a freeze on holding time, unwind to Closed and a full withdrawal. Deployed to Monad testnet on 2026-10-06 and run once against Perpl's testnet exchange: allocate 150 aUSD, open and close 0.001 BTC, withdraw 149.91 aUSD. Then a rule-based agent script (not AI) traded it for about an hour: 10 orders filled, and 5 orders past the $200 cap were refused on chain by the guard.
- 105 contract tests, 64 server tests and Foundry invariant and reentrancy suites, run in CI. Four rounds of internal security review; one found and fixed a bug in registry registration.

WHAT IS NOT BUILT YET

- The live venue is a deterministic mock and the USDC is a mock token. On testnet the price comes from our own keeper. The Perpl vault on testnet has traded only 0.001 BTC for about an hour, with an agent script we wrote.
- Differential privacy covers public data only. The reporter adds Laplace noise to clipped returns from public marks, so it demonstrates the mechanism and the on-chain budget, not protection of anything private. The stated epsilon is exact for the mean only. Batch intents are visible in settlement calldata.
- The demo's batcher and reporter are our own server, which keeps claim proofs in memory. No external audit has been done.
- The guard limits behaviour. It does not make a strategy good and it does not prevent losses inside the mandate. Realised loss can exceed the drawdown term by slippage and gaps. Leverage and size are checked per order; between orders only the mark-based terms act.

WHY IT IS USEFUL

An allocator reads one term sheet per vault, compares vaults by it, and knows that a violation fails as a transaction instead of turning up in a post-mortem. An agent builder with no reputation gets a way to raise capital that does not require one: launch a tight mandate, lock it, and let the contract carry the trust.
