No wallet, no login and no testnet MON are needed for the main pass. Open the Live product link. The server signs for a demo allocator and the demo agents with testnet-only keys and pays the gas. Every number on the page is a contract read on Monad testnet, and every entry in the execution feed links to its transaction on monadscan.

A five-minute pass:

1. Market. Four seeded vaults, one venue, four different mandates. Each row shows drawdown, leverage and mark age against the limit that vault is locked to. Click the Tight Mandate row (3% drawdown, 10-second mark age).
2. Agent. The term sheet lists every locked term: its value, where the vault stands now and what happens when it is crossed. The hash under it is the termsHash locked before the first deposit.
3. Allocate. Press "Connect" in the header and choose "Demo allocator". Pick 1,000, press "Review allocation", then "approve() + allocate()". Shares are minted at the marked NAV.
4. Live Risk. Press "Send order inside mandate". It passes the guard and appears in the feed.
5. Press "Send over-limit order". The guard refuses it and the feed prints the contract's own custom error. No venue state changes.
6. Press "−2% shock". Tight Mandate runs at about 2x, so it is now about 4% under its high-water mark, past its 3% limit. It reads OVER LIMIT but is not frozen yet.
7. Press "poke()". The vault freezes and the caller is paid the bounty. Press "Send order inside mandate" again: the agent is refused with AgentNotActive.
8. Press "unwind()" up to five times, a few seconds apart. Each call closes a fifth of the position. After the fifth the vault is Closed.
9. Back on Allocate, press "Withdraw all shares". Withdrawals stay open through the freeze.

Optional:

10. Term sheet buttons. On the Agent screen of an Active vault, the button beside a term sends the order that crosses it (wrong market, wrong direction, a limit price too far from the mark, and so on). Each is refused by the guard with its own error.
11. Volatility clause. Press "+3%", then within a few seconds select Range Carry on Market and press "Send order inside mandate" on Live Risk. Its drawdown is inside the mandate, but the clause can refuse new exposure with StressBreach until the market has been quiet for a minute or two. "Send reduce-only order" still passes.
12. Batch. With the demo allocator connected, press "depositEscrow()", then "Sign AllocationIntent". Signing is off-chain. Epochs are 20 seconds long in the demo. When the epoch has ended, press "settleEpoch()". The intents are netted into one deposit per vault. Press "claimShares()" under "Settled, unclaimed shares" to receive them with a Merkle proof.
13. Privacy. Press "postLeaderboard()". The server publishes a signed, noised release of vault statistics to MandateRegistry, and the page re-reads it from the contract before it shows VERIFIED ONCHAIN. The registry adds the epsilon the release spends to a running total and refuses a release past the cap. The slider beside it is arithmetic in your browser and sends nothing.
14. Launch. This one needs a browser wallet on Monad testnet with a little MON for gas, because the vault and its agent are yours. Press "Connect", choose "Browser wallet", then open Launch, pick a preset and press "Launch mandate". One createMandate() transaction deploys the vault, sets and locks its terms and lists it on Market. "Get test mUSDC" in the wallet menu mints test USDC to fund it.

When you finish, "Restore $2000" puts the price back for the next visitor.

Things you may notice:

- The first load can take up to a minute. The host sleeps when nobody has visited for a while, and the first request wakes it. Later loads are fast.
- If the first click after loading reports MarkTooOld, wait about five seconds and try again. The price oracle slows down while nobody is watching and speeds up when a page opens.
- Once a vault has been frozen, the server deploys a fresh set of vaults when the next visitor arrives or leaves, so every judge starts from Active vaults. If the page opens during a reset, the vaults are back in a minute or two. Addresses on the live page can therefore be newer than the ones in the README; the README's addresses and transactions stay on chain.
- The venue and the USDC are mocks, and the price is set by our own keeper. The contracts under test are MandateFactory, MandateVault, MandateRiskGuard, BatchAllocator and MandateRegistry. The Perpl adapter is deployed to Monad testnet separately and has traded small sizes against Perpl's testnet exchange (one round trip, then a rule-based agent script: 10 orders filled, 5 over-cap orders refused on chain, listed in docs/perpl-adapter.md); it is not part of the live book.
- The statistics on the Privacy screen are computed from public vault marks. The screen demonstrates the release mechanism and the on-chain epsilon ledger, not protection of private data.
- The server pays for settlements and releases inside an hourly gas allowance, and a release can be published at most once every two minutes. If a button reports a limit, wait and try again.
- The local build has one thing the live page cannot: the 12-second comparison (the oracle held to one mark per 12 seconds, as a 12-second chain would force, against a 4-second mark-age mandate that then reverts with MarkTooOld). To run it: clone the repository, run npm ci and npm run web with Node 22.14 or newer, open http://localhost:3000, and use the mark-cadence switch on Live Risk.

Contract addresses, a recorded run with transaction links, the AI tool disclosure and the third-party code list are in the repository README.
